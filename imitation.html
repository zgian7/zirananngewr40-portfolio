Section One

Imitation

Before the Piece

This assignment matters because it taught me how imitation can carry emotion. I was not only copying another writer's style; I was studying how rhythm, repetition, intensity, and sentence movement can make a reader feel something before the argument becomes explicit.

Through imitation, I learned that emotion in writing is not only about saying "I feel this." It can be built through another writer's methods: where a sentence slows down, where it becomes sharp, where it repeats, and where the voice creates pressure.

After the Piece

From this assignment, I learned how to borrow a writer's technique in order to communicate my own emotion. Imitation did not mean losing my voice. It helped me understand how another writer's rhythm, line breaks, sentence length, and repeated phrases could become tools for expressing pressure, nervousness, desire, discomfort, or intensity.

This changed my understanding of style. Style is not decoration after meaning; it is one of the ways emotion reaches the reader. By imitating someone else's method, I learned more clearly what I wanted my own writing to make the reader feel.
