Process Work, Cookbooks, and Writing Struggles

Process Reflection

Low-stakes writing helped me build larger assignments because it gave me a place to think before the writing had to be polished. The cover letter below is one example of that process work. It shows me naming what was already working in my Food Fight draft and what still felt uncertain: focus, repetition, scene detail, and structure.

This kind of writing helped because it turned anxiety into a revision plan. Instead of only feeling that the essay was not finished, I could identify what I needed to sharpen and what kind of help I wanted from feedback.

Revised Cover Letter

Food Fight Revision Plan

For this draft, I am most proud of the way the essay turns a feeling into a clearer argument. I think the strongest parts are the moments where I describe the quiet pressure of fine dining: the menu words people pretend to understand, the way people act more carefully at the table, and the feeling that eating becomes partly about proving that you belong.

The part I am most worried about is focus. I know the essay is about fine dining as a performance, but I am still trying to make sure it does not repeat the same point too many times. I also want to make sure the essay does not sound like I am simply attacking expensive restaurants. I do enjoy them, and I want the essay to hold both feelings at once: attraction and discomfort.

I am trying to imitate some of the food writers we have read, especially Anthony Bourdain and Kat Kinsman. From Bourdain, I am trying to borrow the confident, observational style where food becomes connected to culture and attitude. From Kinsman, I am trying to borrow the technique of attaching food to people, so the argument comes through small human moments instead of just explanation.

I think the imitation is starting to work in a few places, especially the moments with the wine pronunciation and the nervous behavior at the table, but I still need to make those scenes feel sharper and more specific.

Museum Label

This process evidence shows revision before the final draft. I was already aware that my essay had a real argument, but I also noticed that it risked repeating the same idea.

The cover letter helped me turn feedback into goals: make the scenes sharper, keep the argument complicated, and organize the essay so each paragraph has a different job.
