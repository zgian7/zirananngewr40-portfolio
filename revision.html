The Performance of Taste

Ziran Ann Ge

The first thing I noticed was not the food. It was the tension.

Not the loud kind. The quiet kind.

The kind that lives in dim lighting, polished silverware, and menus filled with words nobody wants to admit they do not understand. Even after going to many expensive restaurants, I still feel strangely nervous every time I sit down at one.

And honestly, I think many people in those restaurants feel the same way.

Revision Focus

What I Changed After Feedback

  • Made the fine dining scene sharper and more specific.
  • Reduced repetition by giving each paragraph a different job.
  • Kept both attraction and discomfort instead of making a simple attack.
  • Connected small details to the larger argument about taste and performance.
